Why this course is shaped the way it is

Safeguarding is the gate, not a module

Most AI training for teachers covers safeguarding in a slide near the end. This course puts it in front, twice, and will not let a learner past it — the remaining modules are inert until both gates are passed. Then every module after them closes by applying the same discipline to whatever it has just taught.

Gate one · what never goes in

Treat a chatbot as a potentially public data warehouse, not a private tool.

Eight categories of personal data, the combinations that identify a child without naming one, a redaction drill, and the four decisions before a tool touches a classroom.

Gate two · harm, and what you must do

The duty to report is yours in law. It is not delegated by your school.

Wadeema's Law Articles 42 and 43, the escalation ladder beyond your school, AI-enabled harm between children, wellbeing, and concerns about adults.

The argument the course turns on

Every other failure of an AI tool — bias, invented sources, confident error, a task made easier while claiming to be better — is in the output. You read it, reject it, ask again, and nothing has happened. A privacy failure is in the input. It happens the moment you press enter, and there is no second draft. You get unlimited attempts at one and a single attempt at the other, which is why they are not taught as the same subject.

The regulatory and legal framework

What is established in law, and what it covers

Teachers are rarely told which instrument applies to what — so when something goes wrong, the question "who do I tell, and am I obliged to?" gets answered by school custom instead of by law. The course names each instrument so that a teacher knows which question to ask and who to ask it of.

The distinction that gets lost first

KHDA is the regulator. It is not the legislation. It licenses and inspects, and it will ask a school to show its safeguarding arrangements. The duty that falls on a teacher personally comes from federal law, and no school policy — and no inspection outcome — creates or removes it.

Instrument What it establishes What it covers in a school
KHDA Knowledge and Human Development Authority — the regulator for private education in Dubai Regulator Licensing and inspection of private schools in Dubai, carried out through the Dubai Schools Inspection Bureau against the UAE School Inspection Framework. Safeguarding and child protection arrangements are inspected, and in Dubai safeguarding operates as a limiting judgement — it caps what can be awarded above it. In practice an inspection expects to see a child protection and safeguarding policy with its review date, a named designated safeguarding lead and deputy, a confidential concern log, a single central record, an online safety policy, an approved code of conduct, and whole-staff training records with dates.
Federal Law No. 3 of 2016 Child Rights Law — “Wadeema’s Law” Personal duty The rights of the child and the duty to protect them. Article 42 obliges any person who knows that a child is exposed to harm to report it to the appropriate authorities, including the police and child protection units. Article 43 places a specific duty on named professionals — including teachers — to report signs of abuse, neglect or mistreatment. Article 49 provides for child protection units. This is the one that falls on the individual. The obligation is yours; it is not delegated to you by your employer and it cannot be withdrawn by one. “I told my line manager and they said to leave it” does not discharge it. Module 2 teaches the duty, the threshold (concern, not proof), and what you do and never do when a child discloses.
Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 Protection of Personal Data Data The federal data protection regime: a lawful basis for processing, data minimisation, purpose limitation, security, and the rights of the data subject including access and erasure. A child’s name, work, reading age, SEND status or photograph entered into a third-party AI tool is a processing and transfer event, whether or not anything bad follows. Module 1 teaches the eight categories of personal data, the combinations that identify a child without naming one, and the four decisions — approval, consent, minimisation, retention — before a tool touches a classroom.
Federal Decree-Law No. 26 of 2025 Child Digital Safety — in force 1 January 2026, fully enforceable January 2027 Newest Duties on digital platforms, internet service providers and custodians — age verification proportionate to risk, parental control tools, notice-and-takedown for child sexual abuse material, filtering, and reporting to the competent authorities. No processing of the personal data of a child under 13 without explicit custodian consent, and no targeted advertising or commercial profiling of them. Schools are not named as a directly regulated party — which is the most common misreading of it in staffrooms. A school is caught because it chooses the platforms, creates the accounts and sets the year groups, so it has to be able to evidence that it took reasonable steps. Module 2 teaches what that means in practice: a platform register, the under-13 line, and checking a tool’s own minimum age before the lesson rather than after the complaint.

For school leaders and inspection

The evidence it produces

A certificate proves attendance. What an inspector actually wants is the work: rebuilt tasks on your own schemes, student work showing the demand rose, and a staff body that knows where a concern goes.

What a participating teacher produces

  • An honest audit of one task they already set, naming the strategy missing from it.
  • One task in three routes to a single destination, with a statement of where it nearly went wrong.
  • A finished task on their own unit, with six tests applied and the failing one named.
  • A one-page AI use note: approved tools, what never goes in, which tasks will never get an AI step, their DSL and the external reporting route.
Stated plainly

The course's own certificate is self-declared, generated in the learner's browser from a name they type. It is not verified training evidence and should not be filed as proof of safeguarding training. For that you need your own attendance record alongside the learner's exported portfolio, reviewed by a person. The course collects nothing, so it can verify nothing — that trade is deliberate and we would rather say so.
